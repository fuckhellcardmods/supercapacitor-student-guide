Common safety

  • Wear splash goggles, protective clothing/lab coat, closed-toe shoes, and SDS-selected gloves.
  • Know the eyewash, shower, spill kit, extinguisher, shutoff, and nearest exit.
  • Use approved ventilation for ethyl acetate, methanol, ethanol, TMOS, NH4OH, and other volatile chemicals.
  • Keep ignition sources away from flammable solvents and hydrogen work.
  • Label every prepared solution and waste/recovery bottle.
  • Never seal a vessel generating gas.

CSTR - Saponification / Reaction Kinetics

Baffles stay installed. They are internal plates that improve mixing. Students do not remove them, the lid, probes, or tubing.

Make the feeds

FeedHow to make it
2.5 L 0.1 M NaOHSlowly dissolve 10.00 g NaOH in about 2.4 L DI water. Cool, then bring to 2.500 L final volume.
2.5 L 0.1 M ethyl acetateMix 24.48 mL neat ethyl acetate into about 2.25 L DI water, mix vigorously, then bring to 2.500 L final volume.

Run it

  1. Confirm the machine is already assembled. If not, stop and ask.
  2. Fill one feed bottle with NaOH and the other with ethyl acetate. Cap both.
  3. CEM software -> HEATER -> Power On -> Hot Water Circulator.
  4. Set 30 C unless assigned otherwise; PID = Automatic.
  5. Baseline: both pumps = 40 mL/min.
  6. Stirrer = 50%. Start logging.
  7. Wait for steady state, about 30 min.
  8. Record temperature, both flow rates, conductivity, reactor volume, conversion data, and values for k.
  9. Repeat only the assigned temperature and/or unequal-flow conditions. Do not reconfigure the machine.

The manufacturer baffle-removal mixing exercise is intentionally omitted for this course.

PFR - Exercise C Only

No KCl needed. Tracer Exercises A-B are not part of this course.
Do not re-plumb the PFR. The conversion tubing, six-port valve, and sensors should already be installed.

Make the feeds

FeedHow to make it
1.0 L ~0.20 M NaOHDissolve 8.00 g NaOH in about 900 mL DI water. Cool, then bring to 1.000 L final volume.
1.0 L 0.25 M ethyl acetate + dyeMix ~24.4 mL neat ethyl acetate with about 850-900 mL DI water. Add 0.10 g indigo carmine. Bring to 1.000 L final volume.
  1. Confirm all tubing/probes are connected. If anything is loose, stop and ask.
  2. If visible air bubbles are present, stop and ask for the system to be flushed.
  3. Fill and cap both reagent bottles.
  4. Set data collection to 3 s.
  5. Use the instructor-posted pump flow rates.
  6. Press GO and allow about 5 min to reach steady state.
  7. Record flow, temperature, conductivity, and visible color change.
  8. Before bottles run dry, stop logging and pumps.
  9. Flush with distilled water until conductivity is near the distilled-water value.

Hydrogen Generation

High hazard. Concentrated NaOH + aluminum is exothermic and produces flammable H2. Use only the instructor-approved setup. Never seal the vessel.

Per trial: 10.8 g DI water + 8.00 g NaOH + 5.4 g assigned aluminum.

  1. Weigh the dry beaker.
  2. Add water.
  3. Slowly dissolve NaOH with stirring; let it cool to the approved starting condition.
  4. Weigh aluminum.
  5. Add aluminum in the approved hood/collection setup. Do not seal.
  6. Let reaction finish and cool.
  7. Record final mass and observations.
Mass loss is not automatically pure H2; water evaporation can also contribute.

Chemical Battery

Do not put a multimeter in CURRENT mode directly across the battery.

One historical 10-cell build: 10 zinc washers, 10 pennies, 10 cardboard squares, white vinegar, wires, LED/small load, multimeter.

  1. Zinc washer.
  2. Vinegar-soaked cardboard, blotted wet but not dripping.
  3. Penny on top.
  4. Measure voltage.
  5. Repeat the zinc -> cardboard -> penny pattern and record voltage as cells are added.
  6. Connect bottom zinc and top penny to approved leads and test the load.

Biofuel

Instructor must confirm the route. The original brief asks for microalgae extraction; the historical example uses peanut oil + 70% ethanol + NaOH. This guide shows the historical route.

Per trial: 100 g peanut oil + 20 g 70% ethanol + 1.00 g NaOH.

  1. Dissolve NaOH in ethanol.
  2. Heat oil to 60 C while stirring. No flame.
  3. Add alcohol/NaOH mixture. Cover loosely; do not seal.
  4. Run for assigned 40, 60, or 80 min.
  5. Transfer to separatory funnel, cool, and vent safely.
  6. Separate glycerol-rich and biodiesel-rich layers.
  7. Label product, waste, and recovered solvent.

Transparent Silica Aerogel

Fume hood required. Methanol/TMOS/ethanol are flammable or toxic; NH4OH is corrosive/irritating.
Critical-point dryer: trained/authorized operators only.
One batchAmount
Methanol6.315 g
TMOS5.000 g
DI water2.368 g
NH4OH0.090 g
  1. Beaker A: methanol + TMOS.
  2. Beaker B: water + NH4OH.
  3. Combine and stir 30 s.
  4. Pour into clean molds/dishes and cover.
  5. Allow about 5-10 min for initial gel formation.
  6. Age at least 12 h.
  7. Transfer carefully and submerge completely in ethanol for at least 24 h.
  8. Hand off to trained CPD operator.

Instructor / Lab Manager - Reactor Setup

Switching PFR -> CSTR: restore the normal CSTR pump tubing, bypass the PFR six-port injection-valve feed path, connect both pump outlets directly to the two CSTR base feed ports, then install the CSTR lid, probes, stirrer, and baffles before students arrive.
  • Confirm PFR Exercise C pump flow rates.
  • Students should receive both reactors already configured.
  • Pre-stage labeled waste/recovery containers.
