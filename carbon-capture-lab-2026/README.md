# Carbon Capture Challenge
**UTC Chemical Engineering — November 6, 2026 — Student guide**

Welcome! Your group will explore how carbon dioxide can react with alkaline foam and produce a visible precipitate after treatment with calcium chloride.

**Follow the instructor's approved setup and directions. The apparatus is provided preassembled.** Do not change tubing or fittings; leave the jar's exhaust vent open. Wear splash goggles, coat, appropriate gloves, and closed-toe shoes. NaOH is corrosive. Never force the syringe or operate a blocked gas line.

## Materials per group

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Open-cell foam | **ONE cube, approximately 4 × 4 × 3 cm** |
| NaOH soaking solution | **200 mL, 1.25 M** |
| CaCl2 precipitation solution | **200 mL, 0.50 M** |
| Baking soda | **1.00 g** |
| White vinegar (5%) | **15 mL** |
| Apparatus | 125 mL generator flask, connected absorber jar with open vent, syringe interface |
| Recovery | Beakers, forceps, 7 cm filter paper, Buchner funnel, vacuum-rated flask, trap and pump |

## Part A — Gas generation and absorption

1. Note your group number and inspect the **single** foam cube. Record dry foam mass if assigned.
2. Immerse the cube in **200 mL of 1.25 M NaOH** for **3 minutes**, using chemical-resistant forceps or a suitable tool.
3. Drain excess solution using tools. Record wet foam mass if assigned.
4. Place the **drained cube only**, not the soaking solution, into the absorber jar. It must not obstruct the gas inlet or vent.
5. Place the instructor-provided **1.00 g baking soda** into the generator flask.
6. Have the instructor check the preassembled connections, unobstructed vent, and approved pressure/backflow safeguards.
7. Under instructor direction, introduce **15 mL of 5% vinegar** gradually through the approved feed. Observe bubbling; never force the plunger.
8. After vinegar addition is complete, allow **5 minutes** of contact with the jar vent continuously open.
9. When gas generation has stopped and the instructor confirms it is safe, move to Part B.

**Stop and notify the instructor** if connections move, liquid enters the gas tube, the syringe resists, or gas transfer appears blocked.

## Part B — Precipitation and filtration

10. With forceps, transfer the exposed foam into **200 mL of 0.50 M CaCl2**.
11. Allow **5 minutes** of contact; gently press the foam with an appropriate tool.
12. Compress the cube over the same beaker to collect liquid and any suspended white solid.
13. Weigh the completely dry **7 cm filter paper**, before it gets wet.
14. Under supervision, assemble the Buchner funnel, vacuum-rated flask, liquid trap and suction source.
15. Seat the filter paper using a small amount of liquid from the reaction beaker under gentle vacuum; **no separate DI water is planned**.
16. Filter the remaining suspension. Stop and consult the instructor if the filter will not seat.
17. Shut off and safely vent vacuum before removing the funnel or filter.
18. Weigh the **damp filter paper plus collected material**.
19. Report **apparent WET recovery** = (damp paper + material mass) - (original dry filter mass). **This is NOT dry CaCO3 yield or percent yield.** Dispose of liquids and foam in assigned chemical-waste containers.

## Challenge questions

1. Write the reaction between baking soda and vinegar that produces CO2.
2. Explain why NaOH-wetted foam might capture CO2.
3. Why does the jar need an open vent? What could happen if the upstream line is obstructed?
4. Why does CaCl2 solution become cloudy? Does cloudiness alone prove capture?
5. Why is our damp mass not a dry calcium carbonate yield?
